St. John the Baptist · Cle Elum

Our history

From a mission served out of Ellensburg to the brick church on Second Street, a parish shaped by coal, fire, and perseverance.

1880s–1901

Mission years

  • 1886

    The Northern Pacific Railway reaches the upper Kittitas Valley and the coal seams open. Catholic families from Italy, Croatia, Poland, and Ireland arrive to work the mines.

  • 1890s

    Cle Elum’s Catholics gather as a mission of St. Andrew in Ellensburg, worshipping in homes and rented halls whenever a priest could make the journey up the valley.

1902–1920

A church of their own

  • 1902

    The first Catholic church in Cle Elum is built, a frame building raised largely by the labor of mining families.

  • 1914

    The mission becomes an independent parish under the patronage of St. John the Baptist, with its own resident pastor.

  • June 25, 1918

    A catastrophic fire sweeps through Cle Elum, destroying much of the city along with the church and the rectory. The parish is left to begin again.

1921–1999

The brick church

  • 1921

    The present brick church, with its distinctive wooden belfry, is completed. It has served the parish ever since.

  • Mid-century

    Generations of mining families, ranchers, and railroad workers are baptized, married, and buried from this church as the coal industry winds down and the valley reinvents itself.

2000–today

One pastor, two parishes

  • 2000s

    Retirees, remote workers, and young families join the longtime residents in the pews. The parish shares a pastor with Immaculate Conception in Roslyn.

  • 2026

    Both parishes adopt a single shared visual identity: two places, one faith, one future in Christ.
